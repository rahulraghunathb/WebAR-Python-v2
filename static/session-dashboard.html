Feature-Map Session Dashboard

We are doing WebXR world tracking plus worker-side feature-map telemetry. This dashboard visualizes each tracked session of that map-building pipeline. It does not show gaussian splatting output, because this repo is not generating gaussian splats yet.

Back To Tracker
Session history is stored in-memory on the Flask server. The dashboard keeps a bounded history of recent runs so we can inspect world state, target state, map growth, relocalization, and frame/capture behavior per session.

State Timeline?Each stripe compresses the retained session window. Long TRACKING or MAPPED runs indicate stability. Frequent flips usually mean unstable lock or recovery loops.

Compressed session-state stripes across the retained telemetry window.

Map Growth?Keyframes are retained reference views. Landmarks are stable feature points observed across frames. Rising lines usually mean the worker is building a stronger local feature map.

Keyframes and landmarks retained by the worker-side map.

Tracking Strength?Matches are candidate target correspondences, inliers are the consistent subset that supports the solved pose, and tracks are currently maintained visual features. Inliers matter most for a trustworthy lock.

Target matches, inliers, and track counts over the retained session window.

Tracking Quality?These are estimator-health signals. Inlier ratio shows geometric lock quality, observability shows whether the motion and features are informative enough, and long-track ratio shows whether features persist instead of resetting every frame.

Normalized trend view of inlier ratio, motion observability, and long-track ratio.

Map Health?A good map does not just grow. It keeps useful landmarks alive, limits stale state, and shows steady promotion of stable structure over time.

Stable and stale landmark behavior plus map growth-rate signals.

Relocalization Behavior?This shows whether recovery is rare and brief or frequent and expensive. Repeated relocalization usually means the tracker only looks stable between short recovery loops.

Relocalization score, active recovery time, and cumulative attempt/recovery counts.

Baseline Disagreement?These deltas show how much the worker-filtered pose disagrees with the native measurement stream. Rising disagreement can indicate drift, over-filtering, or a bad relocalization lock.

Translation, rotation, and reprojection disagreement trends.

Performance Envelope?Lower frame, capture, and visual-processing times are better. Spikes here often explain why tracking drops into relocalizing or why the capture profile gets throttled.

Frame time, average capture cost, and visual processing time.
